India vs West Indies: Kohli and Gill Centuries Lead India to ODI Win

Virat Kohli and Shubman Gill scored centuries as India chased down West Indies in the first ODI at Thiruvananthapuram on September 27, 2026.

India opened its ODI series against the West Indies with a high-scoring chase on September 27, with Virat Kohli and Shubman Gill both scoring centuries during India's successful pursuit in Thiruvananthapuram. Current match coverage reported a convincing Indian victory after West Indies posted 295 for 7.

West Indies set the target

West Indies produced a substantial first-innings total, putting India's batting under pressure. India's bowlers still created important breakthroughs, with current coverage highlighting Kuldeep Yadav and Prasidh Krishna among the contributors who kept the target within reach.

Gill and Kohli control the chase

India's response was led by Shubman Gill and Virat Kohli. Both reached three figures, giving India the stability needed to chase down the total. NDTV reported that Kohli finished unbeaten on 139 while Gill also completed a century.

The partnership brought together two major parts of India's current ODI batting picture: Gill as a central figure in the newer leadership group and Kohli as one of the format's most experienced run-scorers.

Kohli reaches another milestone

Current reports also noted that Kohli became only the second player after Sachin Tendulkar to reach 15,000 ODI runs. The milestone adds another statistical marker to a career already filled with major ODI records.

Why the result matters

Winning the opening ODI gives India an early series advantage and provides useful information about combinations and conditions. New players can settle into the side while established players gain another competitive match before the next fixture.

West Indies also have information to work with. Their batting showed it could create a challenging total, while India's successful chase demonstrates the importance of taking early wickets before the Indian top order settles.

The wider cricket picture

The series sits inside a busy period for Indian cricket. The 2026 Asian Games are also underway, while preparation and discussion around the 2027 ODI World Cup are already becoming part of the wider cricket conversation. Individual ODIs therefore provide opportunities to evaluate combinations, player workloads and form.

Final takeaway

India's first ODI against West Indies produced a major chase, with centuries from Shubman Gill and Virat Kohli helping India reach the target. Kohli's 15,000-ODI-run milestone added another historical note, while India's bowlers helped keep the West Indies total within reach.

What the chase says about India's batting depth

A successful chase of a substantial ODI target requires more than one big score. It depends on keeping wickets in hand, rotating strike and building partnerships that allow the required rate to remain manageable. The Gill-Kohli performance showed how a stable top order can turn a demanding chase into a controlled one once the partnership becomes established.

For West Indies, the next match offers a chance to adjust the plan. Early wickets can change the shape of an Indian chase, while the visitors can also look to build on the batting positives from the opening game. Bilateral ODI series are valuable precisely because teams can make those adjustments from one match to the next.
