BLACK TORCH Ends After 12 Episodes: Creator Shares Season 2 Hopes

Latest news and confirmed details

HomeAnimeMoviesTechSpaceGaming

BLACK TORCH has reached the end of its first anime run, closing out a 12-episode season after bringing Tsuyoshi Takaki's action manga to the screen. The finale has also opened a new conversation around the show's future, because the available manga material leaves very little conventional source material for a straightforward second season.

According to a September 21 report from ComicBook.com, creator Tsuyoshi Takaki shared hopes that the anime could continue with an original story. That is not the same as an official renewal: there is no confirmed Season 2 release announcement in the report. For now, the interesting development is that the creative door has been left open rather than the story being presented as permanently finished.

The first season reaches its limit

The anime adaptation was unusual from the beginning because the original manga had a relatively short publication history. ComicBook.com notes that the manga ended after only 19 chapters, meaning the television series could adapt essentially the full available run across its 12 episodes.

That situation is important when looking at what comes next. A typical anime continuation can pull new material from an ongoing manga or light novel, but BLACK TORCH does not currently have a large backlog waiting to be animated. A second season would therefore require new source material or an original anime storyline developed for television.

What the finale means for Jiro and Rago

At the center of BLACK TORCH is Jiro Azuma, a young fighter with a shinobi background whose life changes after encountering Rago, a mysterious animal with extraordinary power. The story combines supernatural combat, secret organizations and the relationship between human characters and mononoke.

The final stretch pushes Jiro, Rago and the Bureau of Espionage toward a major confrontation with Amagi. Earlier promotional material from Bushiroad described the final episodes as the climax of the story and highlighted the conflict's personal history as well as the larger battle.

For viewers who followed the anime week by week, the ending therefore functions as both a conclusion to the material that exists and a possible starting point for a new chapter. The distinction matters because an original continuation could explore characters and conflicts that were never covered by the manga.

Why Season 2 would be different

If an original second season is ever produced, it would face a different creative challenge from the first season. Instead of adapting a pre-existing sequence panel by panel, the production would need to establish new story arcs while preserving Takaki's characters, tone and world-building.

That could also give the anime more freedom. A new storyline could spend time on the Bureau of Espionage, expand the supernatural rules around mononoke, or explore what happens to Jiro and Rago after the first season's major conflict. None of those possibilities should be treated as confirmed plot details, however; they are simply the kinds of directions an original continuation could take.

Is BLACK TORCH Season 2 confirmed?

No official second-season release has been confirmed in the current reporting. The confirmed news is that the first anime run has ended after 12 episodes and that the creator has expressed interest in seeing the story continue in some form.

That leaves the next steps with the production side. A future announcement would need to address whether a new project is actually being developed, who would produce it, and whether it would be a traditional sequel, an original story or another format.

What fans should watch next

The end of Season 1 gives BLACK TORCH fans a useful point to watch for official updates. Any announcement from the anime's production committee, broadcaster, publisher or creator would carry more weight than social-media speculation about a renewal.

For now, BLACK TORCH stands as a compact 12-episode adaptation of a manga that had a much shorter publication run than many modern anime sources. Its creator's interest in an original continuation makes the future intriguing, but the status remains unconfirmed.
