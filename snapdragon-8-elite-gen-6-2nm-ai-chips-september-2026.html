Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 Arrives on 2nm: What the New Mobile Chips Change

Latest news and confirmed details

Qualcomm has unveiled its next flagship mobile processors, introducing the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 and Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 on September 22, 2026. Both platforms are built on a 2nm process and target the next generation of premium Android smartphones.

The announcement is important because Qualcomm is combining the move to a smaller manufacturing process with a stronger focus on on-device AI. The company says the new chips are designed for agentic AI experiences, gaming, cameras and connectivity.

Two flagship chips

Qualcomm is taking a two-chip approach this generation. The Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 is positioned as the company's highest-performance mobile platform, while the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 brings many of the same capabilities to a broader group of premium devices.

Both use Qualcomm's custom Oryon CPU architecture, Adreno graphics and Hexagon NPU technology. That combination gives the processors dedicated hardware across general computing, graphics and AI workloads.

Why 2nm matters

The 2nm manufacturing process is one of the headline changes. Smaller process nodes can allow chip designers to increase transistor density and improve the balance between performance and power efficiency, although real-world gains depend on the complete chip design and the device's cooling and power limits.

For smartphones, efficiency is particularly important because every additional performance gain has to operate within a battery-powered and thermally constrained device.

AI moves closer to the phone

Qualcomm is emphasizing agentic AI, meaning software agents that can respond to tasks and adapt to a user's context. The company says the new platforms are designed to make these experiences faster and more personalized.

The AI hardware can also support camera processing, image understanding and other workloads without sending every operation to a remote server. That can potentially reduce latency and improve privacy for certain on-device tasks.

Gaming gets AI-powered graphics

One of the most interesting additions is Adreno Neural Fusion. Qualcomm says the technology uses AI-based Super Resolution and Frame Generation to improve graphics efficiency.

The developer documentation explains that Super Resolution reconstructs a full-resolution image from a lower-resolution input, while Frame Generation creates an intermediate frame between rendered frames. In supported games, that can increase perceived frame rate while reducing the amount of native rendering work.

Which phones will use the chips?

Qualcomm says devices powered by the two new platforms will debut from brands including HONOR, iQOO, Motorola, OnePlus, OPPO, REDMI, RedMagic, vivo and Xiaomi.

That means the announcement is likely to affect a wide range of premium Android phones rather than a single manufacturer's flagship.

What to watch next

The most useful comparisons will come once retail smartphones arrive. Battery life, sustained performance, gaming thermals, camera quality and actual AI workloads will show how the new silicon behaves outside Qualcomm's controlled demonstrations.

For now, the headline is clear: Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 and Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 move Qualcomm's flagship mobile platform to 2nm while putting on-device AI and AI-assisted graphics at the center of the next Android hardware cycle.
