Avengers: Endgame Encore is not simply a second theatrical run of the 2019 movie. Marvel has turned the return of its Infinity Saga finale into a bridge toward Avengers: Doomsday, adding new material, changing parts of the presentation and giving audiences new information about where the MCU is heading.

The timing is important. Endgame originally closed the Infinity Saga in April 2019. Seven years later, Encore is returning to cinemas on September 25, 2026, with additional footage and an exclusive Doomsday look in selected premium presentations. What is actually different in Endgame Encore?

The core 2019 film remains intact. Encore is better understood as an updated presentation wrapped around additional material rather than a completely recut version of Endgame.

  • New introduction: Reports from early screenings describe a short introduction featuring Robert Downey Jr. and Chris Evans reflecting on the impact of Endgame.
  • Additional story material: New footage has been inserted around the ending/credits sequence to connect the film to the current Multiverse Saga.
  • New post-credit material: The premium IMAX/Infinity Vision versions contain additional Avengers: Doomsday-related footage.
  • Visual presentation: Trailer comparisons indicate a more saturated, higher-contrast color grade in parts of the movie.
  • Dialogue adjustment: One Tony Stark line has reportedly been altered from “When you mess with time, it tends to mess back” to “When you mess with time, time tends to mess back.” citeturn1search2

🚨 SPOILERS: The new scenes

1. Loki returns to Steve Rogers and Peggy Carter

One of the reported new scenes revisits the final moment of Steve Rogers' story. Steve and Peggy are together in the alternate timeline where Steve chose to remain after returning the Infinity Stones. A knock interrupts their private moment. Peggy answers the door and encounters Loki, who says he can help them.

This is a major change in how the original Endgame ending can now be viewed. Steve's decision to stay with Peggy was previously presented primarily as emotional closure. Encore potentially turns that timeline into an active part of the Multiverse Saga.

2. Doctor Doom comes looking for Bruce Banner

Another reported scene follows Bruce Banner after the events of Spider-Man: Brand New Day. Banner is reportedly confined while working on controlling his gamma-powered alter ego when Doctor Doom makes contact.

Different early reports disagree on exactly what happens and on Doom's wording. One widely circulated description says Doom tells Banner that he has come to collect him, followed by a burst of gamma energy. Another account describes Doom as appearing to absorb or adapt to Hulk's abilities. Because these details come from audience reports rather than Marvel's official synopsis, they should be treated cautiously. citeturn0search4turn0reddit9

3. The TVA discovers the Multiverse is collapsing

The third major reported sequence returns to the Time Variance Authority. O.B. and Casey are reportedly monitoring incursions between different Earths. Their screens show realities beginning to collide, with the situation escalating beyond a single universe.

The significance is straightforward: Doomsday is not simply another villain-versus-heroes story. The multiverse itself is becoming an existential problem. That fits the direction Marvel has been building through Loki, Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness, Deadpool & Wolverine and The Fantastic Four: First Steps.

4. The Doctor Doom / Iron Man visual connection

Reports also describe a brief sequence involving Doom's armor and the sound of metal being hammered. The sound deliberately recalls Tony Stark constructing his first Iron Man suit in 2008.

That is an especially loaded choice because Robert Downey Jr. is returning to the MCU as Victor von Doom, not Tony Stark. The scene therefore appears designed to make the audience think about the connection between the MCU's original central hero and its new major antagonist without necessarily saying that Doom is Tony.

Why the Encore changes the meaning of Endgame

This is arguably the most interesting part of the re-release. The 2019 version was built as a conclusion. Encore makes portions of that conclusion look like setup.

Steve's retirement with Peggy can now potentially intersect with Loki and the wider multiverse. The final state of Bruce Banner can connect to Doom. The idea of alternate Earths collapsing reframes the MCU's post-Endgame stories as pieces of a much larger crisis.

In other words, Marvel is asking audiences to look backward and reinterpret a movie that was designed to feel final.

So has public opinion on Endgame changed?

Yes, but not in one direction.

The interesting thing about Encore is that it has not produced a broad “Endgame was bad all along” reaction. Instead, the public conversation has split into several distinct camps.

The first camp: “This makes Endgame look better”

A significant group of fans are enthusiastic about the new color grade. Their reaction is essentially that the original movie looked too grey or washed out, particularly during the climactic battlefield sequence. The brighter presentation has made some viewers reconsider a criticism they already had in 2019. citeturn1search3turn1search1

The second camp: “Don't change an iconic movie”

Another group dislikes the idea of changing the visual presentation of a film that already has a well-established identity. Their concern is less about whether the new colors look attractive and more about whether a seven-year-old finished film should be visually revised for a re-release. Creative Bloq reports that this debate has become one of the main talking points surrounding Encore. citeturn1search0

The third camp: “I want the new Doomsday information”

For many MCU fans, the new scenes are the real attraction. Joe Russo has described Encore as “required viewing” for following Doomsday, and Anthony Russo has suggested that even people who saw Endgame in 2019 will not necessarily anticipate what happens next. citeturn0news10

There is also clear resistance to the theatrical strategy. Some fans would rather Marvel release the new footage directly on Disney+ or YouTube instead of requiring another cinema ticket. Has the reputation of the original Endgame fallen?

Not really. What has changed is the conversation around it.

In 2019, the dominant discussion centered on whether the movie successfully concluded eleven years of MCU storytelling. The emotional reaction was enormous: Tony's sacrifice, Steve's ending, Natasha's death, the Avengers assembling and the final funeral became cultural reference points.

In 2026, the conversation is much more technical and retrospective. Fans are asking whether the original color grade was too muted, whether certain plot decisions look different after years of Multiverse Saga storytelling, and whether Encore's new scenes change the interpretation of the ending.

The movie itself has not suddenly become a different film. Instead, the MCU around it has changed. That makes Endgame feel different because the audience now knows that Marvel's supposedly final endings can become doors into other universes and timelines.

What Encore says about the MCU in 2026

The re-release is also a snapshot of Marvel's current strategy. Rather than treating the Infinity Saga as a sealed chapter, Marvel is using it as a foundation for the Multiverse Saga's next major Avengers event.

The choice is particularly significant because Doomsday brings together characters and worlds from different eras of Marvel's screen history. The Encore material appears designed to remind audiences that the rules established by earlier MCU films still matter.

That is why the old Tony Stark line about time is receiving attention. A tiny dialogue adjustment would be meaningless in isolation, but in a movie leading directly into a multiverse story, even a small change can invite viewers to look for connections. Any larger interpretation remains speculation until Marvel confirms it.

Final assessment of Endgame Encore

Endgame Encore has changed the public conversation around Endgame more than it has changed the movie itself.

The biggest shift is that audiences are no longer looking at the 2019 finale only as an ending. Encore turns it into a historical checkpoint in the MCU's larger multiverse story.

The color-grade revision has reopened an old visual debate. The new scenes have created fresh theories about Loki, Steve, Bruce Banner, Doctor Doom and the TVA. The premium-format strategy has created another debate about whether essential story material should be exclusive to certain screens. And the sheer nostalgia of seeing the Avengers assemble again is bringing the emotional side of the 2019 phenomenon back into theaters.

Public opinion therefore appears to be evolving rather than reversing. The broad affection for Endgame remains, but fans now have more reasons to analyze it: its visual style, its place in the Multiverse Saga, its newly added material and its relationship with Avengers: Doomsday.

And perhaps that is the most Marvel thing about Encore. A movie that once told audiences “this is the end” has returned seven years later to say: look again — the story wasn't finished.
