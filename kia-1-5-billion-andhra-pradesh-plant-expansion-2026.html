Kia Reportedly Weighs a $1.5 Billion Expansion of Its Andhra Pradesh Plant

Reports say Kia is considering a major expansion of its Anantapur facility that could lift annual capacity from 300,000 to 500,000 vehicles, but the plan has not yet been formally confirmed.

A large expansion is reportedly under consideration

Kia is reported to be weighing an investment of around US$1.5 billion to expand its manufacturing footprint at Anantapur in Andhra Pradesh. The proposal could increase the plant’s annual production capacity from approximately 300,000 vehicles to as much as 500,000, according to reporting by The Economic Times.

The report says a new production line is part of the proposal and that a formal announcement may be made around the Andhra Pradesh Investor Summit scheduled for the following week.

The distinction between a proposal and a confirmed investment

The plan should not yet be treated as a completed investment decision. The report says a memorandum of understanding had not been signed at the time of publication, while Kia’s response did not confirm the specific expansion details.

That means the proposed amount, capacity target and timing remain subject to formal announcements and approvals. A potential expansion can signal confidence in the Indian market, but it is not the same as construction having started or a new line being commissioned.

What a higher capacity could mean

If the proposal proceeds, additional capacity could give Kia more room to respond to domestic demand and export opportunities. It would also deepen the role of Andhra Pradesh’s automotive manufacturing ecosystem and its supplier network.

The next meaningful milestones will be a formal company statement, a signed agreement, investment phasing and a clear production timeline. Until then, the reported figures are best understood as a plan under consideration.

Insight Forge takeaway: Kia is reported to be considering a $1.5 billion Anantapur expansion that could lift capacity to 500,000 vehicles a year. The proposal was not formally confirmed in the cited report.
