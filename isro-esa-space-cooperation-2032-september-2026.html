ISRO and ESA Extend Space Cooperation Through 2032: Human Exploration and Space Weather Added

India's space programme and Europe's space agency are extending their long-running partnership. The European Space Agency (ESA) and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) have agreed to continue their Cooperative Agreement until January 8, 2032, while also expanding the areas covered by the framework.

ESA announced the renewal on September 21, 2026, saying the agreement was extended through an exchange of letters signed in Paris during the International Space Summit. The updated framework keeps existing cooperation in place and adds several priorities that are becoming increasingly important as space activity expands.

What is changing?

The renewed agreement now explicitly includes cooperation on space weather, human and robotic exploration and the sustainability of outer space activities. Existing collaboration areas such as Earth observation, satellite navigation, space science and mission operations support remain part of the broader framework.

That combination is significant because it connects scientific research with future exploration. Space weather affects satellites, communications and human operations in space, while exploration cooperation can involve technologies and research needed for missions beyond low Earth orbit.

A partnership that goes back decades

ESA notes that its relationship with ISRO spans more than two decades under the current Cooperative Agreement. The original framework entered into force in January 2002, building on a much longer history of technical and scientific links.

During that time, the agencies have worked together across multiple areas. The partnership has included Earth-observation activities, navigation support, space-science programmes and operational assistance for missions.

The new extension therefore does not create a relationship from scratch. Instead, it updates an existing framework so the agencies can pursue newer priorities while maintaining the cooperation already in place.

Why space weather matters

Space weather is driven by activity from the Sun and can influence the space environment around Earth. Strong events can affect satellites, radio communications, navigation systems and other infrastructure that depends on reliable space-based services.

For two agencies involved in large Earth-observation and space-science programmes, expanding collaboration in this area can support better measurements, modeling and information sharing. The agreement itself does not announce a single new mission, but it gives both organizations a formal basis for pursuing future projects in the field.

Human and robotic exploration

The addition of human and robotic exploration is another important part of the update. ISRO is developing India's human-spaceflight capabilities, while ESA is involved in international exploration programmes and technology development.

Cooperation can take many forms, including technical studies, research experiments, astronaut-related activities, robotics, communications and mission support. The updated agreement establishes the wider area of cooperation, while individual projects would still require their own arrangements and approvals.

Outer-space sustainability

The new framework also recognizes the growing importance of keeping orbital environments usable over the long term. Satellites, launch vehicles and other spacecraft operate in increasingly crowded regions of space, making coordination, space situational awareness and responsible operations more important.

For ISRO and ESA, including sustainability in the updated cooperation framework creates room for future work that addresses these challenges without limiting the partnership to one specific mission.

What happens next?

The practical impact of the renewal will depend on the projects that follow. ESA says the renewed agreement is intended to support scientific discovery, technological innovation and the sustainable use of space.

For India and Europe, the extension keeps a long-standing channel for cooperation open through 2032 while broadening it to include some of the major challenges and opportunities facing the space sector. The next milestones will come from concrete missions, research programmes and technical collaborations carried out under the renewed framework.
