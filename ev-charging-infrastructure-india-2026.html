India EV Charging Infrastructure 2026

New public stations, fast chargers and the push toward a connected national network

India’s EV charging network is entering a new phase in 2026, moving beyond a handful of metro cities toward a wider national infrastructure layer. According to the Ministry of Heavy Industries, data from Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited showed 52,718 public charging stations in India by July 2026, including 16,561 stations equipped with fast chargers for cars. The government has also allocated ₹2,000 crore under the PM E-DRIVE scheme for public EV charging infrastructure across the country, with a focus on cities and highways.

One important change is the growing mix of locations. Charging is no longer limited to dedicated EV hubs. Public chargers are being added at fuel stations, shopping areas, transport locations, workplaces, residential developments and highway amenities. This matters because EV ownership depends on more than the number of plugs installed. Drivers need chargers where they actually stop during daily routines and long-distance trips.

The PM E-DRIVE programme is designed to support a pan-India charging ecosystem. Government guidance also treats EV charging as an unlicensed activity, allowing private companies and other eligible operators to participate. The Ministry of Power’s 2024 charging-infrastructure guidelines are intended to improve standards, interoperability and safety. Battery swapping and charging infrastructure are also covered by newer national guidelines.

The regional picture shows why a national approach is important. Official FAME-II data reported in March 2026 listed large numbers of chargers installed through oil marketing companies in states such as Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Rajasthan. These figures are not directly interchangeable with the broader BHEL total because they come from different programmes and reporting systems, but together they show how charging infrastructure is spreading across multiple parts of the country.

Private investment is adding another layer. Tata Power, for example, says its charging business is expanding across cities, towns, highways, fleet locations and residential sites. Its FY2026 report highlighted more than 700 EZ-ready cities and towns and a network of public, semi-public and fleet points. Other charging operators are also building networks around high-traffic urban and intercity routes.

The next challenge is reliability. A charging station is only useful when it is available, compatible with the vehicle and easy to locate and pay for. India therefore needs more attention to uptime, grid capacity, connection quality and convenient digital access. The government’s e-Amrit platform also provides a way for users to locate charging stations.

For EV buyers, the result is a gradually changing travel map. Metro areas are gaining density while highway and Tier-II/Tier-III connectivity is becoming increasingly important. India’s charging story in 2026 is no longer simply about installing more chargers; it is about creating a dependable network that connects everyday travel with intercity mobility.
