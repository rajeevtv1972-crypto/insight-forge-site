One Piece Anime Ends 2026 Run After Episode 1180: What the 2027 Return Means

One Piece has finished its 2026 anime run after Episode 1180 and is set to return in 2027. Here is what the schedule change means for Elbaph.

One Piece has reached an unusual point in its long television history: Episode 1180 has now aired, and the TV anime is finished for 2026. Current reporting says the series will return in 2027, following the September 27 release of Episode 1180. The change is part of a broader move toward a more controlled annual schedule as the anime adapts the Elbaph Arc.

Episode 1180 closes the 2026 run

Episode 1180 serves as the final One Piece TV episode of the year. Anime Corner reports that the series returned after a three-month break on April 5 and has now completed its 2026 broadcast schedule. Viewers who expected another run of weekly episodes through December will instead wait for the 2027 return.

Why the anime is slowing down

One Piece is one of television anime's longest-running productions, and its schedule has historically required the adaptation to stay close to the manga. A slower release pattern gives the source material more room to remain ahead while giving the anime team additional production breathing room.

Recent scheduling information has pointed toward roughly 26 episodes per year, compared with the larger annual totals One Piece historically delivered. That is a significant change for a series associated with near-weekly broadcasting.

Elbaph is still the central story

The schedule change does not mean the Elbaph Arc is ending. The anime is pausing after another major installment in the arc. Elbaph has been part of One Piece mythology for years, making its animated exploration one of the series' major current storylines.

Current reporting also notes the anime debut of Loki's voice in this part of the story, adding another important character to the expanding Elbaph cast.

What happens during the break?

For viewers, the immediate change is the disappearance of the regular Sunday episode slot. The break also gives audiences more time to catch up, revisit earlier Elbaph episodes and follow the manga separately. Because One Piece discussions move quickly online, a pause can also make it easier for viewers on different schedules to avoid spoilers.

Crunchyroll and Netflix remain different

One Piece does not have one universal global release calendar. Crunchyroll's simulcast schedule generally places new episodes earlier, while Netflix can release the same material later depending on territory. That difference becomes especially important around major episodes, when social media discussion starts as soon as the earliest audience watches.

Why the change matters

The 2026 schedule is another sign that long-running anime production is changing. Instead of maintaining a continuous weekly broadcast, studios can use limited annual runs to create more production breathing room. For One Piece, that approach could make individual episodes more deliberate while preserving distance between manga and anime.

Final takeaway

Episode 1180 is the end of One Piece's 2026 TV run, not the end of Elbaph. The anime is scheduled to continue in 2027, with the slower annual rhythm giving the production more room to adapt the manga. Fans now have a natural catch-up point before the next phase begins.

What fans can do before 2027

The pause creates a useful opportunity to revisit the arc with a different perspective. Viewers can compare the anime's treatment of Elbaph with the manga, revisit earlier introductions and keep track of the characters whose roles are becoming more important. It is also a good point to organize a spoiler-free watch list, particularly for viewers who use Netflix and receive episodes later than the Crunchyroll simulcast.

For a series this large, a break can also help new viewers. Someone who starts now can work through earlier arcs without immediately having to follow a weekly schedule. By the time the 2027 episodes arrive, more viewers may be ready to experience the next stage together.
