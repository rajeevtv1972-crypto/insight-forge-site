SpaceX CRS-35: What NASA’s October Station Resupply Will Deliver

Cargo missions keep the orbiting laboratory running

Space · Published 2026-10-09 · Insight Forge

NASA and SpaceX are targeting an October 13 launch for a cargo mission carrying supplies and science investigations to the ISS.

A delivery run for the station

NASA has announced a target of 6:33 a.m. Eastern Daylight Time on October 13, 2026, for SpaceX’s 35th Commercial Resupply Services mission. Launch targets can change with weather, vehicle readiness and range availability, so check NASA’s updates before treating the time as final.

What cargo missions carry

A resupply spacecraft can deliver food, crew supplies, spare parts and scientific investigations. Payloads may include equipment for experiments involving biology, materials or other processes under microgravity. Regular cargo flights support daily operations as well as research schedules.

Why logistics matter

Every kilogram launched must fit mass, volume, power and handling limits. Cargo is packed around the crew’s schedule and station storage. Some experiments need temperature control or rapid transfer after arrival, making mission planning more complex than sending a box into orbit.

What happens after launch

After reaching orbit, the spacecraft performs maneuvers to approach the station. Depending on the vehicle and mission plan, the crew may use a robotic arm to capture and berth it. Controllers verify the spacecraft is secure before unloading begins.

Watch official coverage

NASA publishes launch updates and mission explainers through its official website. If a launch is delayed, rely on the revised schedule rather than reposted countdown graphics. The payloads’ work aboard the station is the mission’s longer-term value.
