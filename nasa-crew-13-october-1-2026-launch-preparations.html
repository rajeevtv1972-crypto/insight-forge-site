NASA and SpaceX Target October 1 for Crew-13: Final Preparations Underway

NASA and SpaceX are preparing for Crew-13, with teams targeting October 1, 2026 as the earliest launch opportunity for the next crewed Dragon mission to the International Space Station. NASA's September 22 update says the target is 11:10 a.m. EDT and that final prelaunch activities and reviews are underway. citeturn0search7

Crew-13 is part of NASA's continuing effort to maintain regular crew transportation to the station. The mission will use a SpaceX Dragon spacecraft launched by a Falcon 9 rocket, with teams checking the spacecraft, rocket and station systems before the launch window.

What happens before launch?

NASA says teams are completing a series of prelaunch activities and intensive reviews. These reviews are important because a crewed launch requires coordination between the launch vehicle, spacecraft, astronauts and the space station.

Engineers must also account for weather, range conditions, vehicle readiness and station traffic. A target date is therefore not the same thing as a guaranteed launch time. NASA can adjust the schedule when conditions do not meet its flight requirements.

Why Crew-13 matters

The International Space Station depends on regular crew rotations to keep research and operations running. Astronauts conduct scientific experiments, maintain hardware and support the station's long-term operations.

NASA's recent station updates say the Expedition 75 crew is preparing for a crew swap while conducting research including advanced health technologies. That places Crew-13 within a broader sequence of crew transportation rather than as an isolated launch. citeturn0search7

SpaceX's expanding NASA role

Crew-13 also arrives as NASA continues to rely heavily on SpaceX for astronaut transportation. Reuters reported on September 18 that NASA awarded SpaceX another $946 million for three additional ISS crew missions, bringing the total number of NASA astronaut flights under the contract to 17 and the contract value to $5.92 billion. citeturn0news9

The expanded agreement is part of NASA's strategy to maintain regular access to the ISS while preserving more than one U.S. crew-transport option. SpaceX currently operates Crew Dragon missions, while NASA continues work involving Boeing's Starliner program.

What to watch next

  • Final flight-readiness reviews
  • Weather and range conditions
  • Dragon and Falcon 9 preparation
  • Station traffic and docking readiness
  • The final launch decision before October 1

For now, October 1 remains the earliest targeted opportunity rather than a guarantee. NASA's prelaunch process is designed to resolve technical and operational questions before astronauts fly.
