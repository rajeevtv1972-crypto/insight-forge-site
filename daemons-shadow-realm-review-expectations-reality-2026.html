Daemons of the Shadow Realm Review: Expectations vs Reality

What fans wanted from Hiromu Arakawa's new anime and what the episodes delivered

Home Anime Movies Tech Space Gaming

Daemons of the Shadow Realm had a very specific challenge before its April 4, 2026 premiere: its creator is Hiromu Arakawa, the manga creator best known for Fullmetal Alchemist. The anime is directed by Masahiro Ando and animated by BONES FILM, with two consecutive cours announced.

What people expected

For many viewers, the expectation was not simply “another fantasy anime.” Arakawa's name created expectations for strong character writing, an unusual world and a story capable of mixing action with grounded human problems. A spring preview from The People's Movies described the anticipation around the adaptation as extremely high and found the first episode immediately compelling.

There was another expectation: surprise. The premise follows twins Yuru and Asa, whose connection to powerful supernatural Daemons is tied to a conflict that quickly changes the meaning of their quiet mountain life. Critics going into the premiere knew very little by design, and several reviews emphasized that the less a viewer knows about the first episode, the stronger the effect of its twists can be.

How it actually went

The opening was widely received as a strong start. Rotten Tomatoes lists an 8.5/10 critic review for Episode 1 from But Why Tho?, which praised the blend of fantasy with realistic settings and political or military pressure. Another review from The People's Movies called the premiere one of the strongest it had seen in some time and highlighted the desire to continue immediately after the credits.

The reception remained solid as more episodes arrived. AniGems' July 15 consensus data shows a global score of 8.32/10, with MyAnimeList at 7.88/10 from about 34,900 voters, Filmarks at 4.1/5 and Gamer at 5.0/5. Its episode scores on MyAnimeList gradually eased from 8.04 for Episode 1 to 7.88 for Episode 13, which suggests the early shock value was not the only reason viewers stayed engaged.

Where the anime met expectations

The biggest success is its ability to change tone without abandoning the characters. The premiere starts with a quiet village setup, then rapidly expands into a much larger conflict. Reviews repeatedly noted the unusual transition and the sense that the story had far more underneath its surface than the initial premise suggested.

The supernatural system also has a character-focused center. The Daemons are not just generic monsters; the twins' connection to them creates a relationship between family history, identity and power. That gives the action a reason to matter beyond spectacle.

Where expectations became more demanding

The Arakawa comparison is impossible to avoid for viewers familiar with her earlier work. That does not mean the new series needs to repeat Fullmetal Alchemist, but it does mean every new character and twist is examined closely. Some viewers may also prefer the story's mystery-first structure to reveal its answers faster, while others enjoy the gradual expansion of the world.

Because the anime was announced as a two-cour production, the full judgment on the adaptation's larger story remains incomplete while the 2026 run is still unfolding. The available reception data, however, shows that the premiere generated strong interest and that the series maintained substantial audience engagement through its early run.

Final takeaway

Daemons of the Shadow Realm did not rely only on the Arakawa name. The first episodes gave viewers a strange, serious supernatural story that deliberately plays with expectations. The strongest public response so far has centered on the opening twist, character dynamics and the feeling that the story is building toward something larger. Its ongoing release means the final assessment is still developing, but the early evidence shows that the adaptation generated a substantial response rather than fading after the premiere.
