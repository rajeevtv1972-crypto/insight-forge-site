Stranger Things Complete Series Review: What Critics Loved, What Divided Them, and Did the Finale Work?

Critics' consensus, season-by-season analysis and finale review

Stranger Things has now completed its five-season run, making it possible to judge the Netflix phenomenon as a whole rather than only one season at a time. The series began in 2016 as a compact 1980s-set mystery about a missing boy, a secret laboratory and a strange girl with supernatural abilities. By the final season, it had become a much larger science-fiction horror franchise with enormous production values, a sprawling ensemble and a mythology built around the Upside Down.

To compile this review, I compared the available critic aggregates and published reviews from outlets including Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, The Guardian, Variety, Ars Technica, BBC and other publications. The result is a picture that is more interesting than simply calling the show a hit or a disappointment: critics have consistently praised its characters, performances, atmosphere and ability to create event television, while the later seasons have attracted more criticism for their scale, mythology, repetition and increasingly long runtimes.

The overall critical picture

Rotten Tomatoes currently lists 90% from 662 critic reviews for the series, with an 82% audience score from more than 50,000 ratings. Its season-by-season Tomatometer scores are 97% for Season 1, 94% for Season 2, 89% for Season 3, 89% for Season 4 and 83% for Season 5. Season 1: the formula that made Stranger Things special

The first season remains the clearest expression of the show's original identity. Hawkins is small enough to feel mysterious, the characters are young enough to make the adventure feel dangerous, and the supernatural story is balanced with friendship, family drama and a strong sense of 1980s genre nostalgia.

Critics responded strongly to that mixture. Contemporary reviews highlighted the show's combination of scares and emotional beats, while Metacritic's review archive includes praise for its fun, frightening atmosphere and convincing recreation of the era. The high Rotten Tomatoes score of 97% reflects how broadly that first-season formula connected with critics. citeturn0search0turn0search13

What works especially well is restraint. The mystery of the Upside Down is introduced gradually, and the emotional stakes are understandable even before the mythology becomes complicated. The children are not merely miniature action heroes; their friendship is the engine of the story.

Season 2: darker, more emotional, still focused

Season 2 expanded the mythology without completely abandoning the intimacy of the first season. The Mind Flayer threat made the supernatural world larger, but the season continued to spend time on Eleven, Will, Hopper and the rest of the Hawkins group.

With a 94% Tomatometer score and a 78 Metacritic score, the second season remained strongly received. citeturn0search0turn0search1 Its main strength was character continuity: the consequences of Season 1 mattered, and the characters were allowed to change rather than simply reset for another adventure.

The season also demonstrated one of Stranger Things' most important strengths: it understands that horror works better when the audience cares about the people being threatened. The monsters are memorable, but the relationships are what make the danger meaningful.

Season 3: bigger, brighter and more openly entertaining

Season 3 changed the texture of the series. The setting became more colorful, the Starcourt Mall became a major location, romance and comedy became more prominent, and the story leaned harder into blockbuster spectacle.

The critical reception remained positive overall, although the aggregate scores dropped from the first two seasons. Rotten Tomatoes lists 89%, while Metacritic lists 72. citeturn0search0turn0search1

This is where the series' strengths and weaknesses started to become easier to see. The characters were still highly watchable, the action was more ambitious and the music-driven 1980s presentation was extremely confident. At the same time, the plot could feel more engineered around spectacle than the relatively contained mystery of Season 1.

Season 4: the blockbuster era

Season 4 was the moment Stranger Things fully became a television blockbuster. Episodes became much longer, the cast was divided across several locations, the horror became more graphic, and Vecna gave the mythology a humanoid central villain.

Rotten Tomatoes gives Season 4 an 89% Tomatometer score and describes it as darker and denser than its predecessors. Metacritic lists a 69 score, with reviews praising the scale while questioning aspects of the expansion. citeturn0search2turn0search4

That balance is important. Season 4's best moments are not necessarily its biggest battles. The character scenes work because the enormous supernatural plot is repeatedly brought back to personal fears, friendships, grief and growing up.

There was also a clear criticism: the ensemble had become so large that following every storyline could make the season feel scattered. Rotten Tomatoes' compilation of early reviews specifically highlighted the difficulty of checking in with all the characters, while still noting the strengths of the performances and mythology. citeturn0search3

Season 5: the final chapter divides the conversation

The fifth season had an unusually difficult job. It had to resolve the Upside Down story, close character arcs built over nearly a decade and deliver the emotional farewell expected from one of Netflix's defining shows.

The early critical response was very positive. Rotten Tomatoes' first-review roundup described the opening episodes as thrilling and entertaining, with critics praising the action, horror, relationships and emotional payoff. citeturn0search5 But the reception became more mixed as the full season and finale were evaluated.

Rotten Tomatoes now lists Season 5 at 83% from 169 critic reviews and a 52% audience score from more than 10,000 ratings. Its critic consensus praises the season's genre entertainment, while the audience response is substantially more divided. citeturn0search12

Its review praised the decision to reconnect the ending with the show's childhood roots and noted that the final battle delivered spectacle and emotional moments, while arguing that the franchise's mythology and continuing expansion made the idea of a clean ending complicated. citeturn0search7

What the critics consistently praise

Characters and relationships: Across the series, the strongest common thread is the ensemble. Eleven, Hopper, Joyce, Steve, Dustin, Lucas, Mike, Will, Nancy, Robin, Max and the wider group give the supernatural story an emotional center.

Performances: The cast has grown with the show, and critics repeatedly point to the commitment of the performers. The later seasons especially gave the actors larger emotional and dramatic material.

Atmosphere: The combination of 1980s references, synth-heavy music, horror imagery and small-town Americana remains one of the series' clearest signatures.

Event television: Stranger Things understands how to make individual episodes feel like occasions. The long runtimes, cliffhangers, set pieces and season finales helped turn the series into a global conversation rather than simply another streaming drama.

Where the series became weaker

Mythology overload: As the story expanded, explanations became increasingly important. What began as a mysterious alternate dimension eventually required more detailed mythology, villains and rules. Some critics felt that this added complexity without always adding emotional depth.

Too many characters: The original group was easy to follow. Later seasons had multiple teams working in different locations, which allowed more combinations but also reduced the amount of time available for each relationship.

Runtime and scale: Bigger episodes are not automatically better episodes. The show's increasing length sometimes made individual storylines feel stretched, especially when multiple characters had to be moved into position for the final act.

Nostalgia: Nostalgia was initially one of Stranger Things' greatest advantages because it supported the story. In later seasons, some critics felt the references and familiar genre patterns became more visible than the underlying character development. Variety's final-season criticism is a clear example of that concern. citeturn0search10

Did Stranger Things stick the landing?

The review consensus is not a simple yes or no. The final season remains positively reviewed by critics overall, but its scores are lower than the first two seasons, and several major reviews identify problems with the expanded mythology, repetition and scale. citeturn0search0turn0search12

At the same time, the emotional response to the ending is stronger than the criticism of its mechanics. Reviews from Ars Technica and The Guardian both identify genuine emotional value in the farewell, particularly when the story returns to the friendships and childhood identity that defined the beginning. citeturn0search6turn0search7

That distinction explains the most useful way to judge the series as a whole: Stranger Things is more successful as a character-driven coming-of-age adventure wrapped inside science-fiction horror than as a perfectly engineered mythology. Its biggest moments work because viewers spent years with these characters. Its weaker moments usually appear when the mythology becomes more important than the people.

Final review

As a five-season series, Stranger Things is an unusually ambitious piece of mainstream television. It evolved from a relatively contained mystery into a huge franchise without losing its central emotional idea: childhood friendships can make an impossible world survivable.

The critical record reflects that evolution. Seasons 1 and 2 remain the strongest consensus performers, Season 3 successfully shifted toward colorful blockbuster entertainment, Season 4 delivered an enormous horror spectacle, and Season 5 provided a conclusion that critics generally appreciated while debating whether the story had become too large and repetitive.

There are plot conveniences, mythology complications and stretches where the runtime works against the story. But the series also delivered memorable characters, major performances, striking horror imagery, an instantly recognizable musical identity and a rare ability to make audiences care about a fictional town for nearly a decade.

Insight Forge verdict: Rather than reducing Stranger Things to a single score, the critical record points to a series whose greatest achievement is the emotional connection between its characters and audience. Its final chapter is imperfect, but the journey from the mysterious first season to the closing farewell remains the reason the show became such a defining Netflix phenomenon.
